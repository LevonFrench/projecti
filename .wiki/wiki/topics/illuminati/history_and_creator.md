# Steve Jackson and the History of the Illuminati Game

This document details the biography of game designer Steve Jackson, the founding of Steve Jackson Games (SJG), and the historical trajectory of the classic *Illuminati* card game. It also highlights the landmark 1990 U.S. Secret Service raid on the company, which served as a primary catalyst for the founding of the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF).

---

## 1. Biography of Steve Jackson (Founder of SJG)

**Steve Jackson** (born 1953 in Austin, Texas) is a pioneer in the American tabletop and role-playing game industry. 

### 1.1 Early Career and Metagaming
Jackson attended Rice University in Houston, Texas, focusing on student journalism and wargaming. In the mid-1970s, he began designing games for Metagaming Concepts. His early successes included:
*   **Ogre (1977)**: A tactical science-fiction microgame about a super-intelligent cybernetic tank attacking a command post. It remains one of his most enduring designs.
*   **The Fantasy Trip (1977–1980)**: An early tactical role-playing game system that laid the groundwork for his future RPG designs.

### 1.2 Founding Steve Jackson Games (1980)
Seeking full creative and financial control over his work, Jackson left Metagaming and founded **Steve Jackson Games** in 1980. He purchased the magazine *The Space Gamer* from Metagaming to serve as the company's voice and community hub. SJG established its offices in Austin, Texas, which remains a key hub for independent tabletop publishers.

## 2. The Genesis of the Illuminati Card Game (1981–1982)

In September 1981, Steve Jackson was reading Robert Shea and Robert Anton Wilson’s 1975 cult classic science-fiction trilogy, ***The Illuminatus! Trilogy***. Inspired by its sprawling, satirical conspiracies, he decided to create a game that captured the "feel" of secret societies pulling the strings of global events.

### 2.1 Design Philosophy
Rather than buying the rights to adapt the novels directly (which would have constrained the mechanical scope to the specific characters and plots of the books), Jackson designed a sandbox of conspiracy theories:
*   **Humor and Satire**: The game was designed to be deeply tongue-in-cheek. It contrasted serious entities like the *C.I.A.* and *K.G.B.* with ridiculous ones like the *Boy Sprouts*, *Trekkies*, and *Cattle Mutilators*.
*   **The Power Structure Layout**: The mechanic of attaching outward-pointing arrows to represent puppet networks was a significant departure from standard board game paths.
*   **Victory and Asymmetry**: Jackson introduced the concept of individual secret win conditions for each faction, promoting table negotiation, bluffing, and sudden betrayals.

### 2.2 Release and Early Success
The game was released in 1982 in the company's signature plastic "Pocket Box" format. It immediately captured the gaming community's attention and won the **1982 Origins Award for Best Boardgame**.

## 3. The 1990 U.S. Secret Service Raid & The EFF

On March 1, 1990, Steve Jackson Games became the center of a federal digital privacy controversy that would permanently alter the history of electronic civil liberties in the United States.

### 3.1 Background of the Investigation
The U.S. Secret Service was conducting an investigation into the leakage of a proprietary Bell South text file detailing the operations of the 9-1-1 emergency system. 
*   **The Link**: Loyd Blankenship (a former member of the hacker group "Legion of Doom" and the writer of the upcoming game supplement ***GURPS Cyberpunk***) was an employee at SJ Games. 
*   **The BBS**: Blankenship ran a Bulletin Board System (BBS) called "Phoenix" from his home and SJ Games' offices, which hosted discussions about computer systems. The leaked Bell South file had been posted to a BBS connected to the Legion of Doom network.

### 3.2 The Raid (March 1, 1990)
Agents raided the offices of Steve Jackson Games in Austin. They seized:
*   Four computers, including the server hosting the Phoenix BBS.
*   Hundreds of floppy disks.
*   The only physical and electronic drafts of the manuscript for ***GURPS Cyberpunk***.

When Steve Jackson went to the Secret Service offices to retrieve his company's assets, agents told him that *GURPS Cyberpunk* was a "handbook for computer crime" and that publishing it was illegal.

### 3.3 Impact and Financial Crisis
The seizure of essential hardware and files forced SJ Games to lay off nearly half of its staff and brought the company to the brink of bankruptcy. *GURPS Cyberpunk* was delayed for months, and the manuscript had to be painfully reconstructed from older backup drafts. It was eventually published in late 1990, proudly bearing the subtitle: *"The book that was seized by the U.S. Secret Service!"*

### 3.4 The Lawsuit and the Founding of the EFF
In response to this raid and similar aggressive actions by law enforcement against electronic bulletin boards:
1.  **Founding of the EFF (July 1990)**: Mitch Kapor (founder of Lotus Development) and John Perry Barlow (lyricist for the Grateful Dead) co-founded the **Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF)** to defend the civil liberties of computer users.
2.  **The Lawsuit**: Assisted by the EFF, Steve Jackson Games sued the U.S. Secret Service in 1991 (*Steve Jackson Games, Inc. v. United States Secret Service*).

### 3.5 The Legal Verdict (1993)
In 1993, the court ruled in favor of Steve Jackson Games, awarding the company over $50,000 in damages and $250,000 in attorney's fees. 
*   The judge ruled that the Secret Service had been negligent, failed to read the privacy protection statutes, and had illegally seized electronic communications (emails stored on the BBS server) under the Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA).
*   **Precedent**: The case established that electronic speech, BBS communications, and email were entitled to the same Constitutional protections as print media and phone calls.
